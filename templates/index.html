04 / FIELD GUIDE

Understand the moving ground.

A compact guide to the science behind slope instability.

01 / THE BASICS
⌁

What is a landslide?

The downslope movement of rock, debris or earth under gravity. It includes falls, topples, slides, spreads and flows. Water often changes the strength and weight of slope materials.

RockDebrisEarth
02 / TRIGGERS
↘

What can trigger one?

Intense or prolonged rain, rising groundwater, earthquakes, river erosion, excavation, slope loading and drainage changes can contribute to failure.

03 / WARNING SIGNS
⌖

What should I notice?

Fresh ground cracks, bulging roads, new seepage, tilting trees or poles, leaning walls, and doors or windows suddenly sticking may indicate movement.

04 / THE SCIENCE
◒

Why does water matter?

Water can increase soil weight and pore-water pressure, reducing effective stress and shear resistance. The response depends on soil, rock, drainage and slope geometry.

SUSCEPTIBILITYWhere could it happen?

Long-term terrain and material conditions.

HAZARDHow likely and when?

Probability, timing, magnitude and intensity.

IMPORTANT SCIENTIFIC NOTE

Screening index ≠ probability.

The current engine is a transparent weighted index with illustrative scaling references. Its bands are not official alert levels, and its score is not the probability of a landslide.

A validated machine-learning model needs verified landslide and non-landslide records, local feature measurements, spatial/temporal holdout testing, calibration and expert review.
